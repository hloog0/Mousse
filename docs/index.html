The Mouse Who Wanted to Glide

On a tidy desk in a slightly messy dorm room lived a mouse named Notch.

Notch was a good mouse. Reliable. Clicky. He had survived three group projects and one very long essay. But every time he turned his wheel, the page lurched. Click. Jump. Click. Jump. Like climbing stairs in the dark.

Beside him, built into the laptop, lived Pad the trackpad. Pad didn't click. Pad glided. Pages poured under Pad like warm syrup, and Pad never let anyone forget it.

“Have you tried,” Pad said one evening, “just being smoother?”

Notch tried. He polished his wheel with lip balm. He spun faster, which only flung the page three paragraphs at a time. He spun slower, which made the page climb the stairs very politely.

Their student squinted at the screen and sighed. “I'll just buy one of those smooth-scrolling apps,” they said. Then they saw the price, and quietly closed the tab.

That night, something small blinked in the menu bar.

“Psst,” it said. “Give me your clicks.”

“All of them?” asked Notch.

“Every one. I'll turn each click into a glide: a gentle push, a soft landing. Spin fast and I'll carry you farther. Stop, and I'll stop with you. Pad can keep doing Pad things.”

“What does it cost?”

“Nothing. I'm free. You can even read my whole recipe.”

So Notch turned his wheel. Click, and the page slid like a sled on fresh snow. Click, click, click, and it flowed like a river that knew exactly where it was going.

Pad was quiet for a long moment. “Huh,” said Pad.

The student stopped squinting. Notch stopped feeling like a staircase. And when Notch stopped spinning, the page stopped too, right where he meant it to.

The end. Now go scroll something.

With SmoothWheel0 clicks

Same story, same wheel clicks. One climbs stairs; the other glides.

Small app. Big difference.

It lives in your menu bar and quietly fixes the one thing your mouse wheel was never good at.

Glides, doesn't jump

Each click becomes a short, eased animation. Keep spinning and the clicks blend into one continuous glide.

Stops when you stop

“Glide after you stop” sets how quickly scrolling settles, from a crisp halt to a long, lazy coast.

Speeds up when you spin

Flick the wheel and it takes bigger steps, so long pages still fly by.

Leaves your trackpad alone

Trackpad and Magic Mouse gestures pass straight through, exactly as before.

Off where you want it off

Switch it off for a single app, or everywhere, right from the menu bar.

Private and tiny

No accounts, no network access, nothing collected. Under a megabyte, and no Dock icon.

Up and gliding in a minute

Four steps, once.

Download SmoothWheel.zip and double-click it to unzip.

Drag SmoothWheel into your Applications folder.

Open it. When macOS says it couldn't verify the app, click Done, then System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

Turn on SmoothWheel under Privacy & Security → Accessibility. Then just scroll.

Why the warning? Apple charges developers a yearly fee to skip it. SmoothWheel is free, so it doesn't pay that toll. Its full source is on GitHub for anyone to read or build.

Questions

Which mice does it work with?

Any mouse with a notched scroll wheel. It was built for and tested with the Logitech Signature M750. If your mouse software has its own smooth scrolling option, turn that off so the two don't stack.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

That's the only way macOS lets an app intercept scroll events and replace them with smooth ones. SmoothWheel only listens to scroll-wheel events. It never sees keystrokes or clicks and never sends anything anywhere.

Is it really free?

Yes, completely. It's open source under the MIT license: free to use, share and modify.

How do I uninstall it?

Quit it from its menu bar panel, drag it from Applications to the Trash, and remove it from System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility.
